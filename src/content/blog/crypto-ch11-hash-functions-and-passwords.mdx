Hashes look simple and cause most of the quiet disasters. This chapter measures avalanche, finds collisions, and puts a number on what password hashing is worth.

## Avalanche is necessary, not sufficient

Flip one input bit and a good hash flips about half the output. Over 2,000 trials, SHA-256, SHA-1 and MD5 each flipped 49.9% of output bits. A byte-sum checksum flipped 6.4%. But CRC32 scored 49.0% and is still useless as a cryptographic hash, because it is linear: the CRC of a XOR b XOR c equals the XOR of the three CRCs, and a test proves it. MD5 and SHA-1 also avalanche perfectly and are still broken by collision attacks.

## Birthday collisions

A k-bit hash collides after roughly 2^(k/2) evaluations, not 2^k. On truncated SHA-256 with 20 bits, the table method averaged 1,252 evaluations against a bound of 1,024. At 32 bits it was 80,637 against 65,536. A cycle-finding version (Floyd) uses constant memory at a cost of a few times more evaluations; with 20 runs per size its numbers are noisy, so treat that as a small constant factor.

## What a password hash is for

Storing SHA-256 of a password is fast, unsalted (equal passwords give equal hashes), and exactly what an attacker wants. On one CPU thread, SHA-256 managed about 1.65 million guesses per second and Argon2id (19 MiB, two passes) managed 44, a gap of roughly 37,000 times. A 100,000-word list falls in 0.06 seconds against SHA-256 and takes 37.5 minutes against Argon2id.

Those are single-thread numbers. Real attackers use GPUs, which speed up fast hashes enormously and memory-hard ones less, so the absolute times flatter the defender. The takeaway is the ratio, plus the usual hygiene: salts, rate limits, and long unique passphrases.

## An integrity tool, and its limit

A small tool writes a SHA-256 manifest of a directory and reports modified, missing and added files. A plain manifest only catches accidents, because an attacker can edit a file and regenerate the manifest. With a key file, the manifest carries an HMAC that an attacker cannot recompute.
